Green Logistics Optimizer

Analyses shipments, finds avoidable fuel, emissions and packaging waste, and recommends greener delivery options. Every number is calculated in this page from the data you give it.

Request

Same shape as the problem statement's API. Edit any value and run it again. The example below is the one given in the statement.

Fields

Required: shipment_id, distance_km, vehicle_capacity_kg, load_kg. Optional: fuel_consumption_litre, packaging_kg, empty_return_km. Without fuel, the score uses utilisation and packaging only and no emission figure is given.

Response

Run an analysis to see the green score and recommendation.

Load your logistics dataset

Paste CSV or choose a file. Columns: shipment_id, distance_km, vehicle_capacity_kg, load_kg, fuel_consumption_litre. Optional: packaging_kg, delivery_location, priority, empty_return_km, delivery_time_hours, cost. The data never leaves your browser.

The format test file is made-up rows that exercise duplicates, invalid values and missing fields. It checks the pipeline. Its results are not evidence of savings.

Assumptions you can change

Defaults are common planning values, not measurements from your fleet. Calibrate them with your own data. Changing them updates every result.

How it works

  • Emissions = fuel litres × emission factor (kg CO₂ per litre of diesel).
  • Utilisation = load ÷ vehicle capacity.
  • Fuel intensity = litres per tonne-km of load. Compared with a reference: your assumption for a single shipment, the dataset median for a fleet.
  • Green score (0 to 100) = 45% utilisation, 35% fuel intensity, 20% packaging. Each part is scored against its target and capped at 1. Missing parts are left out and the weights re-scaled.
  • Consolidation (fleet): shipments to the same delivery_location are packed into as few vehicles as fit, up to the maximum fill. The surviving trip is assumed to use the highest fuel and distance among the trips it replaces.
  • Delivery protection: shipments marked urgent, high or express in the priority column are never merged.
  • Packaging: shipments above the target packaging ratio are brought down to it.
  • Single-shipment savings are rule-based estimates: the gap to target × the consolidation fuel factor. They are estimates, not measurements.

Limits

  • No route coordinates are used, so distance savings come only from removing or merging trips, not from new road routes.
  • Each dataset row is treated as one trip.
  • Emissions use fuel burned only. Packaging production, warehousing and electric vehicles are not modelled.
  • Delivery time is not predicted. Merged shipments are listed so their time windows can be checked before acting.
  • Cost is shown only when a cost column is supplied.
